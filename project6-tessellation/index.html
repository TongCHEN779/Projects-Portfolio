▚ GEOMETRY · WALLPAPER GROUPS

Draw one motif. Let a symmetry group do the rest.

Sketch anything in the little tile on the right — or start from a preset — then choose a wallpaper group. The group is generated from a handful of moves (mirror, glide, quarter-turn), and everything you see is your single motif repeated under every symmetry those moves generate. There are exactly 17 such groups in the plane; eight of them live on the square lattice you're drawing on.

How it works. A wallpaper group is a set of rigid motions of the plane that map the pattern to itself. Rather than hard-coding each group, this page stores only its generators — say a quarter-turn r and a mirror m — and computes the group closure: keep composing elements until no new ones appear, reducing every translation modulo the unit cell. That finite set of matrices is exactly what gets drawn. So p4 = ⟨r⟩ gives 4 copies, p4m = ⟨r, m⟩ gives 8, and p1 = ⟨⟩ gives 1 — the counts you see in the panel aren't typed in, they're derived.

The catch that makes it interesting. Not every combination of moves is a new group: composing a mirror with a translation can hand you back a glide reflection you never asked for, which is why pg and cm exist as separate groups at all. Turn on a few custom toggles and watch the operation count jump to the next legal size — the group closes itself. Of the 17 plane groups, the eight here are those compatible with a square lattice; the remaining nine need a hexagonal or rhombic cell.

Why I built it. Symmetry groups are the friendliest doorway into the algebra I use elsewhere on this site — the same "generate a structure from a few rules and see what it forces" move that drives the moment/SOS hierarchy in Project 3.
